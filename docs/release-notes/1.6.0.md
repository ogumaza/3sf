3SF 1.6.0 plays each track the way a soundtrack album plays it, and its rips have the tags for that. Loops get loop tags, two passes and a 7 s fade. Endings stop at their last sound. Fades follow the curve of most soundtrack albums. The release also rips more of StreetPass Mii Plaza as the games play it. Rip your games again to get the new tags.

3SF rips Nintendo 3DS sequenced music into a PSF-family format. The rips play with the game's DSP firmware on an emulated Teak DSP. 3SF comes with a ripper (`3sfrip`), a renderer (`3sfplay`), a foobar2000 component and an XMPlay plugin. The format is described in `docs/3sf.md`.

The programs run on Windows 10 or later and require no runtime DLLs. To install the component, open the file with foobar2000 or add it under Preferences › Components. To install the XMPlay plugin, unzip `xmp-3sf-1.6.0.zip` and put `xmp-3sf.dll` in XMPlay's folder, or in a folder inside it. Then restart XMPlay. For macOS, see the README for instructions on building from source.

## New in 1.6.0

- **Length tags.** `length` now counts from the first sound: the first sample of the output that isn't 0. A looping sequence gets `length` at the end of its second pass and `fade=7`. Earlier versions gave it a 10 s fade. Two new tags, `loop_start` and `loop_length`, give the start of the loop and the length of one pass. A sequence that ends gets `length` at its last sound and `fade=1`, without the half second of silence that earlier versions added. `docs/3sf.md` describes the tags.
- **Long loops.** The length analysis now measures music for up to 3,600 s. A piece whose two passes take more than 600 s gets both passes and the fade. Earlier versions stopped it at 600 s. A sound effect still stops at 600 s. A sequence still playing at its limit gets a length of 10:00, and `3sfrip` prints "warning: no loop or end found for ...; length set to 10:00".
- **Playback like an album.** By default, the players skip the silence before the first sound, play 0.5 s of silence, then the track as its tags say, and 1 s of silence after it. They also skip any silence at the end of a piece that ends. The foobar2000 component (in Advanced preferences) and the XMPlay plugin (on its options page) have eight settings for this: "Skip silence at the start", "Silence before a track", "Loops", "One pass for long loops", "Fade length", "Silence after a track", "Ignore length and fade tags" and "Skip silence at the end". "Loops" sets the number of passes for a file with loop tags. The settings never change a file.
- **`3sfplay`.** `3sfplay` renders the same way by default. `--keep-opening`, `--start-silence`, `--loops`, `--one-pass-over`, `--loop-fade`, `--end-silence`, `--ignore-tags` and `--keep-ending` change that. `--raw` renders a file as it is, with no skip and no added silence.
- **The fade's curve.** Fades on most soundtrack albums follow about the square of the time left in the fade. The players now fade that way. Earlier versions faded linearly. The foobar2000 component and the XMPlay plugin have a setting for the curve, and `3sfplay` takes `--fade-exponent`. The exponent goes from 0.5 to 4: 1 fades linearly, and 2 is the default.
- **Files without a length tag.** By default, such a file now fades over 7 s in place of 10 s. After the sound ends, the players add 1 s of silence in place of half a second.
- **Find Mii II's walls.** In Find Mii II, a room with a wall of fire, ice or poison plays its battle music quieter and muffled until a hero breaks the wall. `BGM_DEN2_Battle01` and `BGM_DEN2_Battle02` now get one more file each in that state, named with `_wall`. The new tag `3sf_lpf` gives the low-pass that the game sets on the sound.
- **Ninja Launcher's jingles.** Ninja Launcher sends its two sequenced jingles to its reverb on bus A. Their rips now do the same.
- **Flower Town's sound test.** Flower Town's 29 `SOUNDLIST_` pieces play only in the Plaza's sound test. The sound test plays them dry. Their rips now play dry too. Earlier versions gave them Flower Town's reverb.
- **Sound archives without names.** Where a sound archive's string block is missing or damaged, `3sfrip` now names each sequence after its label in the sequence file. Earlier versions named it `SEQ_<index>`. A sequence whose file has no label for it keeps that name. The name gives the file name, the title and `3sf_sound`, and `--only` and `--bgm` match it.
- **Tags from the input.** For a game with a driver profile, the `game` tag is now the value of `--game`, or else the name of the image or folder, in both modes. Earlier versions wrote the profile's name, "Pokemon X (Japan)". A game-mode library is named after the same text. Ultimate Angler's two catch sounds no longer get a `comment` tag.
- **Messages.** `--bgm` that finds no music now prints "error: no sequence is music (--bgm)". With `--only`, it prints "error: no music matches '...'". `3sfrip` now ends the line of each `.mini3sf` with the times of its first sample and of its first sound over -72 dB. A warning names a file whose first sample comes more than 0.1 s before its first sound over -72 dB.
- **Other consoles' archives.** `3sfrip` now names the console of a Wii U or Switch sound archive by its byte order. It prints "error: X is a Wii U sound archive (big-endian FSAR); 3SF rips 3DS sound archives (CSAR, .bcsar)" or "error: X is a Switch sound archive (little-endian FSAR); ...". Earlier versions printed "a Wii U or Switch sound archive (FSAR)" for both.
- **Damaged game code.** `3sfrip` decompresses a game's compressed code with ntdlib. The messages for a damaged `.code` stay the same. A few damaged files now decompress, and a file whose footer gives a size over 256 MiB is refused. Good images rip the same.
- **Rips in older players.** Players of 3SF 1.5.1 and older ignore `loop_start` and `loop_length`, and count `length` from the start of the output. 3SF 1.5.0 and 1.5.1 ignore `3sf_lpf` and play the `_wall` files without the low-pass. 3SF 1.4.0 and older refuse the `_wall` files. Rips of earlier versions have no loop tags. The "Loops" setting plays them as tagged.
- **The libraries.** 3SF now builds on ntdlib 7.1.0, with xsflib 1.12.0. The ripper and the players share more of their code with them.

## Features

- **Ripping.** `3sfrip` rips a decrypted 3DS game (`.3ds`, `.cci`, `.cxi` or `.cia`, or an extracted game folder) or a sound archive (`.bcsar`, with a DSP firmware file) into a `.3sflib` and a `.mini3sf` for each sound, or only the music with `--bgm`. A game's rips contain the DSP firmware that the game uses. The firmware can be in a file or in the game's code. `3sfrip` finds the group files that a game keeps outside its sound archive. A piece that a game plays as several sequences gets one `.mini3sf`. `3sfrip` plays each sound to determine its length, fade and loop tags. It also supports drag and drop.
- **Two rip modes.** Game mode runs the game's sound code. Game-mode rips play as the game does. Game mode requires a driver profile for the game. Pokémon X (Japan) has the only one so far. Archive mode plays a sound archive from any game with 3SF's model of the SDK's sound player.
- **Effects.** Game mode plays the reverb that Pokémon X starts in the field. Archive mode plays the effects that the games in its table put on the aux buses, including the DSP firmware's delay and reverb.
- **Starts.** Archive mode rips the listed sequences of known games as the game's code starts them, and their later states as separate files.
- **Rendering.** `3sfplay` renders rips to WAV or FLAC at the DSP's native 32,728 Hz. By default, it renders them the way a soundtrack album plays its tracks.
- **foobar2000.** The component plays `.3sf` and `.mini3sf` files using their length, fade and loop tags. It supports tag editing, ReplayGain, endless playback and quick seeking. It plays each track the way an album does. During playback, the component renders ahead. Seeking within the buffered audio is instant.
- **XMPlay.** The plugin plays `.3sf` and `.mini3sf` files using their length, fade and loop tags. It supports endless playback. It plays each track the way an album does. Like the component, it renders ahead during playback.
- **Speed.** On 64-bit Windows, the DSP firmware is translated into x86-64 code during playback. The 32-bit component and the XMPlay plugin interpret the firmware. Interpreting is slower. Use the 64-bit component if your foobar2000 and its other components allow it.

## Known limitations

- Seeking ahead of the audio buffer waits for rendering to reach the target. A long seek soon after a track starts can still be slow.
- Game mode only has a profile for Pokémon X (Japan). Other games rip in archive mode. Archive mode's model comes from Pokémon X. A game with another version of the sound library may play differently from the console.
- Only sequences are ripped. Use tools such as vgmstream for BCSTM and AAC streams. Wave sounds are skipped. So are sequences whose data is outside the sound archive and its group files, or in a group file that `3sfrip` can't find.
- Archive mode plays effects only for the games and archives in its table. Another release or build of them gets none until someone reads its code. A loose `.bcsar` of another game and the other games get none either.
- Some games pick their effects for each scene or stage. A rip plays every sound with the effects that the game picks most often. Ocarina of Time 3D, Majora's Mask 3D, Island Tour, Star Rush and Super Mario 3D Land pick them that way.
- In one of its modes, WarioWare Gold also puts a pitch shifter on bus A. The rips leave it out.
- A game's code can change a sequence while it plays, such as its tempo or the volume of its tracks. A rip plays the start that the game gives the sequence, and the listed later states. Other changes aren't ripped.
- `3sfrip` finds a piece of several sequences only by the labels that Mighty Gunvolt Burst gives its sequences. In another game, each of a piece's sequences rips alone.
- The output is stereo.
